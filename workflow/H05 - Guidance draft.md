# Draft guidance for answering H05

**Status: draft for review.** General workflow: [[how-to-0-to-1]]. Project answer: [[questions/H05 - What are the risks]].

## What H05 should establish

H05 identifies **what could prevent the project from delivering its intended result**, which concerns matter most and what remains uncertain about them.

## 1. Describe concrete failure scenarios

Start from the objective in H01 and assumptions in H03. For each candidate risk, explain what failure would look like and how it affects the project. A general label becomes useful when its consequence is specific.

Discuss the list with the owner. In our case, all five candidates were retained, but only insufficient useful findings and excessive integration work were considered primary concerns. The remaining issues were considered manageable.

## 2. Separate severity, probability and manageability

- **Severity:** the impact if the issue materializes or remains unresolved.
- **Probability:** the estimated likelihood, with the event and conditions defined.
- **Manageability:** confidence that corrective work can address the issue.

Specify whether probability concerns occurrence or persistence after corrective work. Our answer uses the latter. An issue may occur frequently while rarely remaining a material obstacle.

Manageability alone does not establish low probability. Treat confidence in corrective work as a provisional judgment and record its basis. Keep Unknown where no useful estimate is available; qualitative ratings need no invented percentages. High severity and low probability can coexist.

## 3. Rank concerns according to the project's priorities

Discuss which outcomes are central. The owner placed useful findings above expert-effort savings, leading us to reorder the risks by severity. We then numbered them sequentially and updated the references.

Retain manageable risks where they remain relevant. Their presence records a considered issue; prominence should reflect the agreed assessment.

## 4. Keep the answer compact

Our final table uses: **risk, failure scenario, severity and probability**, with numbering for discussion. We removed the early-check column at the owner's request. Investigations or mitigation details can remain in supporting notes where needed.

## Readiness check

The answer is sufficient to proceed when consequences are concrete, primary concerns are visible, probability has a clear meaning and unassessed items remain explicit. The owner reviews the assessment. Acceptance of the answer leaves the underlying risks and research questions open until evidence or decisions resolve them.
